# Interview

Ask one batch before the run. Use exactly the first 3 questions; add at most 3 optional questions when needed.

## Unskippable

1. What decision will this research inform?
   - Recommended answer: "Decide whether and how to act on [topic]."
2. What does success look like in one paragraph?
   - Recommended answer: "A concise answer with source-backed claims, caveats, and next actions."
3. What would change your mind?
   - Recommended answer: "A primary source, contradictory evidence, or a failed citation check."

## Optional, cap 3

4. What source types are disqualified?
5. What date range or jurisdiction matters?
6. Should this be a scan, check, or dive?
7. What must be filed for future reuse?
8. What budget or deadline applies?

Do not keep interviewing after the run starts unless a blocker appears. Clarification loses value once execution is underway.

## When to skip — and how to say so

Skip the batch **only** when the request already answers all three unskippable questions. A written brief, a CortexOS task packet, or a carefully-written prompt frequently does; a one-line request almost never does.

**A skip must be declared.** One line, naming what supplied the answers:

> Skipping the interview — the brief already states the decision (whether to build X), what success looks like (a filed, source-verified comparison), and what would change your mind (a primary source contradicting the vendor claim).

This is not ceremony. An undeclared skip is indistinguishable from having forgotten the step, so the operator cannot tell a judgement call from a defect — and that is exactly how the step went missing on the first real run of this skill.

**Partial answers are not a skip.** If the request answers two of three, ask the third and only the third.

**Dispatched workers skip by default.** A worker executing a brief has no human in the loop. It does not ask; a missing brief field is a stop condition.
